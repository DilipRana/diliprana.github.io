Selected Publications
October 2026 | IEEE Transaction on Power Electronics
Cryogenic Testing Platform for Power GaN HEMTs - Part I: DC Static Characterization
Purushottam Khadka, Dilip Rana, Tian Qiu, Zheyu Zhang
Abstract: Power electronics applications are expanding beyond conventional limits, and operation across wide temperature ranges is increasingly required for emerging applications. Among power devices, GaN HEMTs have shown great promise for such applications, with some characterization data available at these temperature ranges. For actual converter design, self-characterization of devices at these temperatures may be necessary, but details of suitable characterization platforms are often not provided in the literature. In this paper, we develop a static characterization platform for GaN HEMTs capable of operating across cryogenic temperatures, targeting emerging low-temperature power electronics applications. The platform's automated characterization approach is first discussed, highlighting the platform's operation and capability. Next, the design considerations required to achieve extreme temperature operation, including the thermal considerations, interconnection, and PCB design for the cryocooler setup, are discussed. For characterization accuracy, the design of platform components to allow multiple sample characterization along with the configuration of characterization equipment is discussed. Finally, the platform's capabilities are validated through physical implementation, demonstrating its operational capability by characterizing four different device technologies (SP-GaN, GIT, cascode, and direct-drive), with six samples of the SP-GaN device and four samples each of the GIT, cascode, and direct-drive devices, over a temperature range of 50–400 K.

March 2026 | IEEE PEASA Workshop
Blocking-State Reliability Characterization of GaN HEMTs for Lunar Power Applications
Dilip Rana, Purushottam Khadka, Tian Qiu, Zheyu Zhang
Abstract: Gallium nitride (GaN) high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) have shown strong potential for lunar power applications due to their superior performance demonstrated through various characterization studies. However, their blocking-state reliability under lunar operating conditions remains uncertain, as existing reliability models predict severe degradation at cryogenic temperatures. In this paper, we present the blocking-state reliability characterization of commercial GaN HEMTs under lunar-relevant operating conditions. The reliability test platform and methodology are first presented, including two distinct temperature profiles to evaluate device reliability during lunar night operation and the lunar day–night cycle. The results from both tests demonstrate that the blocking-state lifetime of GaN HEMTs significantly exceeds the predictions of existing reliability models, indicating that existing models are not directly applicable to such extreme temperature conditions. Under thermal cycling conditions, only one of three samples failed, providing valuable insight into the influence of blocking-state stress on overall device reliability in lunar-relevant environments.

March 2026 | IEEE APEC Conference
Reliability Characterization of GaN HEMTs under Extreme Temperature Cycling for Space Power Applications
Dilip Rana, Purushottam Khadka, Tian Qiu, Zheyu Zhang
Abstract: Gallium nitride (GaN) high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs) are attracting increasing attention for future space power applications due to their ability to operate at cryogenic temperatures. While GaN reliability is gradually being explored, studies specifically focused on space applications, where operating temperatures can fluctuate significantly from cryogenic to high temperatures, remain largely unexplored. This paper investigates the thermal cycling reliability of commercial 200 V GaN HEMTs by applying thermal cycles between 50 K and 400 K, mimicking lunar day-night cycle, and performing characterization throughout the stress cycles. The characterization data were analyzed to track parameter shifts and cycles to failure, which were also used to perform Weibull fitting and quantify uncertainty ranges. The results reveal negligible gradual degradation followed by abrupt failure, a behavior not commonly reported in prior GaN thermal cycling studies. The observed failure pattern was further examined and found to involve two distinct events: drain-source breakdown and gate control loss, while freewheeling conduction remained nearly unaffected. In addition, a supplementary test was performed to evaluate the effects of both characterization and thermal cycling on device failures, and the observations are reported.

October 2025 | IEEE ECCE Conference
A Universal Reliability Platform for GaN HEMTs with Realistic Stress Emulation, Online Monitoring, and In-Circuit Characterization
Dilip Rana, Tian Qiu, Zheyu Zhang
Abstract: Realistic emulation of application operating conditions and effective degradation tracking are essential for accurate reliability assessment of power semiconductors. For gallium nitride (GaN) high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs), even minor modifications to the gate and power loops-introduced for online monitoring or offline characterization-can significantly alter stress dynamics due to the high sensitivity of circuit par-asitics at fast switching speeds. This paper presents a universal reliability test platform for GaN HEMTs, applicable across a wide range of power electronic applications, that accurately emulates application-level stressors while supporting both online degradation monitoring and a novel offline in-circuit characterization approach. An overview of the test platform is first presented, covering its implementation and control strategy. The normal operation mode is then described alongside the real-time degradation tracking methodology. A circuit-based offline characterization method is subsequently presented, enabling extraction of I-V characteristics directly from the device within its operating circuit without hardware modifications. Finally, the platform is experimentally validated using commercial GaN HEMTs stressed in a synchronous buck converter configuration, confirming its accuracy and practicality for both online monitoring and offline characterization.
